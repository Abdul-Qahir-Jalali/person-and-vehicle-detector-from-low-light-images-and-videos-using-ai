ABSTRACT
DeepFake Audio Detection for Urdu is an AI-driven system designed to identify and differentiate between authentic and cloned Urdu speech using advanced deep learning techniques. The project focuses on addressing the growing threat of synthetic speech manipulation, particularly in low-resource languages like Urdu, where research and datasets are limited.
To build a robust dataset, real Urdu audio samples were collected from diverse YouTube sources, covering various accents, genders, and speaking styles. Synthetic (fake) audios were then generated using speech-to-speech cloning techniques, resulting in a balanced dataset of 9,600 audio samples, 4,800 real and 4,800 cloned.
For detection, the project leverages Facebook’s Wav2Vec2-Large-XLSR-53, a state-of-the-art self-supervised speech representation model, fine-tuned specifically for Urdu audio deepfake detection. A Flask-based web application was developed to make the system interactive and practical. Users can upload an audio file, which is first processed through ElevenLabs’ API for noise and music separation, then analyzed by the fine-tuned model to predict whether the speech is real or cloned, along with a confidence score.
This system provides an effective, scalable, and language-specific approach to combating voice cloning attacks, contributing valuable research toward deepfake detection in the Urdu language domain.

Introduction

A Flask-based web app was developed for practical use, where users can upload an audio file that is first cleaned using ElevenLabs’ API for noise separation and then analyzed by the model to determine whether it is real or fake. Background and Technologies
Deepfake audio has become a major concern in the digital age, as artificial intelligence can now generate human-like speech that is nearly indistinguishable from real voices. Using advanced machine learning models, cloned voices can mimic tone, accent, and emotional variation with high accuracy. This poses serious ethical and security risks, including, misinformation, impersonation, and identity fraud. The
“DeepFake Audio Detection for Urdu” addresses this challenge by leveraging deep learning models to identify AI-generated speech in the Urdu language. Since limited research exists for Urdu, this project contributes by building a new dataset and fine-tuning pre-trained speech models to improve detection performance in low-resource linguistic environments.
2.1 Deepfake Audio and Speech Synthesis
Machine learning (ML) plays a critical role in the SafeNet system. It provides advanced detection capabilities by analyzing web traffic and identifying patterns indicative of threats.
2.1.1 Overview of Deepfake Technology
The term deepfake originates from the combination of deep learning and fake, describing synthetic media created using neural networks. In the context of audio, deepfakes are generated through AI models that can synthesize a person’s voice from small audio samples. These models use deep neural architectures to learn vocal patterns and reproduce them with remarkable accuracy.
Audio deepfakes are often produced using Text-to-Speech (TTS) or Speech-to-Speech (S2S) systems. TTS systems convert written text into synthesized speech, while S2S systems directly transform one person’s speech into another’s voice. Although these technologies have beneficial applications such as in accessibility tools, dubbing, and virtual assistants they also pose serious threats when misused for deception, impersonation, or fraud.
2.1.2 Evolution of Speech Cloning and TTS Systems
Over the past decade, speech synthesis technology has advanced rapidly due to the availability of large datasets and improved deep learning architectures. Early systems relied on concatenative and parametric synthesis, which produced robotic and unnatural voices. With the introduction of deep neural networks and models like Tacotron, WaveNet, and VITS, speech synthesis reached a new level of realism.
Recent methods have further evolved to speech-to-speech cloning, where the voice of one individual can be transferred onto another’s speech in real time. These techniques capture detailed acoustic and prosodic features, making it extremely difficult for humans to distinguish synthetic audio from real speech. In multilingual contexts, however, research has been mostly limited to high-resource languages such as English and Mandarin, leaving a significant research gap for languages like Urdu.
2.1.3 Challenges in Detecting Deepfake Audio
Detecting deepfake audio presents several challenges. Synthetic voices often share similar spectral and temporal properties with real human speech, making manual detection almost impossible. Additionally, modern cloning systems adapt quickly to detection algorithms, producing ever more convincing results.
The challenges are intensified in low-resource languages like Urdu, where limited datasets and pre-trained models are available. This scarcity of linguistic resources restricts the training of robust detection systems. Moreover, cloned audios may vary in quality depending on the model used, the duration of samples, and recording environments—all of which add complexity to the detection process. Hence, a specialized, Urdu-focused approach is required to ensure effective detection and generalization across diverse speakers and accents.
2.2 Machine Learning for Audio Deepfake Detection
Machine learning (ML) plays a central role in detecting deepfake audios by automatically identifying subtle differences between real and synthetic speech. ML models learn to recognize patterns in speech signals that are often imperceptible to humans, such as artifacts, unnatural harmonics, or inconsistencies in frequency modulation. These models can be trained using supervised or unsupervised learning methods, depending on the availability of labeled data.
In this project, a supervised approach is used, where the model learns from a balanced dataset containing real and cloned Urdu audios. The main goal is to teach the model to classify an input audio as either real or fake with high confidence.
2.2.1 Feature Extraction and Representation in Speech
Feature extraction is one of the most critical steps in deepfake audio detection. Speech signals are complex, containing both linguistic and acoustic information. To effectively analyze them, the audio must be converted into a numerical representation that preserves key patterns of human speech. Traditional methods used handcrafted features such as Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs), spectral contrast, and chroma features to represent sound.
However, recent advancements have shifted toward deep representation learning, where neural networks automatically learn meaningful features from raw waveforms. The Wav2Vec2 model, for instance, extracts high-level contextual embeddings that capture both phonetic and emotional nuances of speech. These embeddings serve as the input for classification layers that distinguish between genuine and cloned voices. This approach eliminates the need for manual feature engineering and significantly improves the accuracy and robustness of the detection system.
2.2.2 Deep Learning Architectures for Speech Analysis
Deep learning has revolutionized the field of speech processing by enabling models to automatically extract and interpret complex features from audio data. Unlike traditional machine learning techniques that depend on handcrafted features, deep neural networks can learn hierarchical representations directly from raw signals.
In deepfake audio detection, architectures such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), and Transformer-based models have shown significant success. CNNs capture local temporal features of audio, while RNNs model sequential dependencies and speech rhythm. However, the introduction of Transformer architectures, such as Wav2Vec2, has set a new standard by learning contextual embeddings from unlabeled audio data at a massive scale.
Wav2Vec2-Large-XLSR-53, developed by Facebook AI, extends this capability across multiple languages, making it ideal for fine-tuning on low-resource languages like Urdu. Its self-supervised pretraining allows it to understand phonetic patterns and nuances, which are crucial for detecting subtle differences between real and cloned voices. In this project, Wav2Vec2’s deep contextualized embeddings serve as the backbone for classifying whether an Urdu audio sample is genuine or synthetic.
2.2.3 Challenges in ML-Based Deepfake Detection
While machine learning offers powerful tools for detecting synthetic speech, it also presents challenges. One major issue is data quality and diversity—models trained on limited or biased datasets may struggle to generalize to unseen speakers or recording conditions. Additionally, deepfake generation techniques evolve rapidly, producing audios that can bypass traditional detection patterns.
Another challenge is model interpretability. Deep learning models often behave as “black boxes,” providing accurate results without clear explanations for their decisions. This limits the ability to understand what specific features the model uses to differentiate between real and cloned speech.
Lastly, computational complexity poses a limitation. Large pre-trained models such as Wav2Vec2 require significant processing power and memory, which can hinder real-time deployment. Despite these challenges, fine-tuning pre-trained models remains a promising and efficient strategy, especially for underrepresented languages like Urdu.
2.3 Existing Solutions and Research Gaps
2.3.1 Existing Deepfake Detection Systems
Several research efforts have been made to address the problem of audio deepfake detection, primarily focusing on English and a few other high-resource languages. Popular approaches include AASIST (Audio Anti-Spoofing Integrated System) and RawNet, both of which leverage neural architectures to distinguish real from synthetic speech. These systems analyze spectral and phase-based inconsistencies that are often present in generated audio.
Additionally, challenge benchmarks such as the ASVspoof Challenge have significantly contributed to the development of deepfake detection systems by providing datasets and evaluation protocols. Models trained on these datasets have achieved remarkable accuracy in detecting TTS and VC (voice conversion) based spoofs.
However, most of these solutions are language-dependent and rely on datasets like English VoxCeleb or LA (Logical Access) corpora. This limits their transferability to other languages such as Urdu, which have distinct phonetic and prosodic features.
2.3.2 Limitations of Current Approaches
Existing detection systems often face limitations when applied to real-world Urdu data. The lack of large-scale Urdu datasets restricts effective model training, leading to overfitting and poor generalization. Furthermore, many models are trained only on text-to-speech (TTS) fakes, while speech-to-speech (S2S) deepfakes—arguably more realistic—remain underrepresented in datasets.
Another key limitation is the environmental variation in real-world recordings. Background noise, multiple speakers, and low-quality microphones can significantly affect detection accuracy. Current systems are also computationally demanding, requiring powerful GPUs for both training and inference, which limits their accessibility for low-resource environments.
These challenges highlight the need for a specialized, optimized solution for Urdu that can handle both TTS and S2S deepfakes effectively—an objective achieved in this project through dataset expansion, data cleaning, and fine-tuning of a robust multilingual model.
2.4 Technologies Overview
The implementation of “DeepFake Audio Detection for Urdu” integrates several modern technologies that collectively enable dataset processing, model fine-tuning, and real-time web-based prediction. The following subsections describe the key technologies used in the project.
2.4.1 Facebook Wav2Vec2-Large-XLSR-53
Wav2Vec2-Large-XLSR-53 is a state-of-the-art self-supervised model developed by Facebook AI (now Meta AI). It is trained on 53 languages and over 56,000 hours of speech data, making it one of the most powerful universal speech representations available. The model learns audio patterns by predicting masked segments in raw waveforms, enabling it to understand complex acoustic features without labeled data.
For this project, Wav2Vec2 is fine-tuned on a custom Urdu dataset containing both real and cloned audios. Fine-tuning allows the model to specialize in detecting subtle differences between natural and synthetic Urdu speech while retaining its broad multilingual understanding. The model outputs confidence scores indicating the likelihood that an input audio is real or fake. Its strong contextual learning ability makes it highly suitable for low-resource languages and real-time inference.
2.4.2 Flask Framework
Flask is a lightweight Python web framework that serves as the foundation of the project’s user interface. It allows seamless integration between the trained machine learning model and the web-based front end. Flask handles the file upload, preprocessing pipeline, and model inference steps through RESTful API routes.
The framework’s simplicity and flexibility make it ideal for deploying machine learning applications. In this project, Flask manages user interactions, processes incoming audio files, and returns detection results in real time. It also supports modular scalability, enabling future extensions such as speaker identification or multi-language detection.
2.4.3 ElevenLabs API
The ElevenLabs API is employed in this project for high-quality noise and music separation. When users upload an audio file, it is first sent to ElevenLabs’ API, which isolates clean vocals by removing background sounds, music, and environmental noise. This preprocessing step ensures that the audio passed to the detection model focuses solely on the speech content, which significantly improves accuracy.
The API’s advanced voice separation algorithms preserve the clarity and timbre of the speaker’s voice, ensuring minimal distortion during the cleaning process. By integrating ElevenLabs into the pipeline, the project achieves a professional-grade preprocessing stage that rivals studio-level voice isolation.

System Design
Designing an effective and intelligent system for detecting deepfake audio in the Urdu language requires a structured and modular architecture. The system must efficiently manage dataset processing, noise separation, feature extraction, and model inference while providing a responsive and user-friendly interface.
The proposed DeepFake Audio Detection for Urdu system is designed with scalability, flexibility, and accuracy in mind. Its architecture integrates deep learning–based classification with modern web technologies to provide real-time analysis of uploaded audios. This chapter explains the architecture, components, and data flow of the system that together enable accurate and practical deepfake audio detection.
3.1 Architecture Overview
The architecture of the proposed system is modular, consisting of distinct yet interconnected components that together form a complete detection pipeline. The system integrates backend deep learning modules, preprocessing APIs, and a Flask-based web interface for real-time interaction.
The architecture is designed to ensure smooth data flow between modules, from user input to model prediction, while maintaining efficiency and reliability. The major components include the Flask web server, ElevenLabs noise separation API, audio preprocessing module, fine-tuned Wav2Vec2 detection model, and the user interface for displaying results.
3.1.1 High-Level Design
The high-level design of the system focuses on seamless communication between preprocessing, model inference, and user interaction modules. When a user uploads an audio file, it is first cleaned using ElevenLabs’ API to remove background sounds and music. The cleaned audio is then preprocessed and passed to the fine-tuned Wav2Vec2-Large-XLSR-53 model, which predicts whether the input is real or fake along with a confidence score.
The results are returned to the Flask server, which displays them to the user through a web interface. Each component operates independently yet contributes to the overall workflow, ensuring modularity, maintainability, and scalability.
3.1.2 Architectural Layers
The system architecture is divided into three primary layers to enhance clarity and maintainability:
Presentation Layer: This layer consists of the web interface built with Flask templates (HTML, CSS, and JavaScript). It allows users to upload Urdu audio files, trigger the detection process, and view results in a simple and interactive way.
Application Logic Layer: This is the core functional layer, hosting the model integration, preprocessing logic, and ElevenLabs API interaction. It manages all internal operations — from noise removal to model inference — and returns predictions to the interface.
Data Layer: This layer handles dataset management, including training, testing, and fine-tuning data. It ensures efficient access and retrieval of stored audio files and supports retraining if future model updates are required.
3.2 Component Details
Each component in the DeepFake Audio Detection system serves a specific purpose in the data processing pipeline. Together, these components form an end-to-end detection system capable of analyzing and classifying Urdu speech samples.
3.2.1 Flask Server
The Flask server acts as the central control unit of the system. It manages incoming requests, processes uploaded audio files, interacts with the ElevenLabs API, and passes the cleaned audio to the deep learning model for prediction. Flask also returns real-time results to the user interface.
Its RESTful structure allows easy communication between the frontend and backend. Furthermore, Flask ensures modular integration making it easier to update the model, modify preprocessing techniques, or expand to additional languages in the future.
3.2.2 Elevenlabs API Integration
The ElevenLabs API plays a crucial role in preprocessing. It removes background noise, music, and other unwanted sounds from uploaded audio files, ensuring the model receives only clean, high-quality vocals.
This step is essential because deepfake detection relies on fine-grained voice characteristics, which can be masked by noise. By integrating ElevenLabs, the system ensures consistent preprocessing across all samples, improving model performance and reliability.
3.2.3 Audio Preprocessing and Feature Extraction
After noise removal, the cleaned audio undergoes preprocessing, where it is normalized, resampled, and trimmed to a consistent format. The preprocessing module ensures that all inputs meet the model’s expected parameters (such as sampling rate and duration).
Feature extraction is automatically handled by the Wav2Vec2 model, which transforms raw waveforms into rich contextual embeddings. These embeddings capture unique vocal features such as pitch, rhythm, and spectral energy that help distinguish real voices from synthetic ones.
3.2.4 Wav2Vec2 Detection Model
At the heart of the system lies the fine-tuned Facebook Wav2Vec2-Large-XLSR-53 model. This transformer-based model has been trained on the custom Urdu dataset consisting of both real and cloned audios. During inference, the model receives the preprocessed waveform and outputs a binary classification: Real (Human) or Fake (AI-generated), along with a confidence score.
Its large-scale multilingual training and contextual understanding make it ideal for Urdu deepfake detection, as it effectively captures subtle inconsistencies introduced by voice cloning models.
3.2.5 User Interface
The web interface provides an interactive platform for users to test audios easily. Developed using Flask templates, HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, it allows users to upload files, view progress during processing, and receive clear output with confidence levels.
The interface displays the result in a simple yet informative way indicating whether the input audio is genuine or AI-generated  making the system accessible to users from both technical and non-technical backgrounds.
3.3 Real-Time Processing and Communication
Unlike traditional offline models, the proposed system is designed for real-time detection, ensuring fast response and user feedback. This responsiveness is crucial for practical deployment in journalism, media verification, and digital forensics..
3.3.1 Real-Time  WorkFlow
Once a user uploads an audio file, Flask immediately forwards it to the ElevenLabs API for cleaning. The cleaned output is processed and fed into the fine-tuned model for classification. The prediction, including the confidence score, is sent back to the Flask server, which instantly displays it to the user. This near real-time loop ensures the system can handle multiple user requests efficiently, maintaining both speed and accuracy.
3.3.2 Data Exchange Patterns
The data exchange in the system follows a clear, linear pattern:
User Input: The user uploads an audio file via the web interface.
Preprocessing: Flask sends the file to ElevenLabs for cleaning and then performs preprocessing steps.
Model Inference: The preprocessed audio is passed to Wav2Vec2 for prediction.
Response Generation: The model returns classification results and confidence scores.
User Output: Flask renders these results on the interface, completing the detection cycle.
This structured data flow ensures efficient communication among modules and allows easy debugging and future scalability.

The data flow in DeepFake Audio Detection for Urdu illustrates how information moves through various stages  from audio input to detection output.
3.4.1 Data Flow for DeepFake Detection

The implementation phase transforms the system design of DeepFake Audio Detection for Urdu into a practical, functioning application. 4.1 Machine Learning Model
The machine learning model forms the analytical core of the DeepFake Audio Detection system. It processes Urdu audio inputs, extracts acoustic and phonetic patterns, and classifies them as either real (human) or fake (AI-generated). The implementation process included dataset preparation, preprocessing, model fine-tuning, evaluation, and integration.
4.1.1 Model Overview
The system utilizes a fine-tuned Wav2Vec2-Large-XLSR-53 model from Facebook AI. This transformer-based speech model was chosen for its multilingual capabilities and superior performance on low-resource languages like Urdu.
The model performs binary classification to distinguish between real and synthetic voices based on learned acoustic representations rather than handcrafted features.
Key steps in implementation included:
Importing the pretrained Wav2Vec2 model and tokenizer using the Hugging Face Transformers library.
Fine-tuning the model on the custom Urdu dataset comprising real and cloned voice samples.
Saving the trained model for later inference within the Flask application.
This approach allows the system to automatically detect subtle inconsistencies in speech characteristics introduced during AI-based voice cloning.
4.1.2 Dataset Selection
The dataset used for model training and evaluation was custom-built, focusing exclusively on the Urdu language to ensure cultural and linguistic relevance. The data sources included:
Real Voice Samples: Genuine Urdu audio collected from native speakers.
Synthetic/Deepfake Samples: Generated using advanced text-to-speech and voice cloning platforms such as ElevenLabs, Descript, and Coqui.ai.
Each audio file was labeled as real (0) or fake (1). The dataset underwent multiple preprocessing steps, including:
Normalization: Adjusting volume levels for uniformity.
Resampling: Converting all audio files to 16 kHz mono format.
Trimming: Removing silence segments at the start and end of each file.
Balancing: Ensuring an equal number of real and fake samples to prevent bias.
The final dataset was split into 80% training and 20% testing sets to ensure reliable model evaluation.
4.1.3 Training and Evaluation
Training was conducted in a controlled environment using PyTorch and Hugging Face Transformers. The process involved:
Feature Extraction: The Wav2Vec2 tokenizer converted raw waveforms into input embeddings automatically, eliminating the need for manual feature engineering.
Fine-Tuning: The pretrained model was fine-tuned on the Urdu dataset for several epochs using the AdamW optimizer, a learning rate scheduler, and cross-entropy loss function.
Training parameters included:
Batch size: 4
Learning rate: 1e-4
Epochs: 20
Gradient accumulation: Enabled to handle large model weights
Evaluation Metrics:
Model performance was evaluated using standard classification metrics:
Accuracy: Percentage of correctly classified audio samples.
Precision: Proportion of true positive predictions among all positive predictions.
Recall: Ability to identify all actual fake samples.
F1-Score: Harmonic mean of precision and recall for balanced performance evaluation.
The fine-tuned Wav2Vec2 model achieved high accuracy and strong generalization on unseen Urdu audio, confirming its effectiveness for deepfake detection.
Confusion matrices were also generated to visualize true and false predictions for real and fake samples.

4.1.4 Model Optimization
To enhance the model’s efficiency and prevent overfitting, several optimization strategies were applied:
Learning Rate Scheduling: Adaptive learning rates were used to stabilize convergence.
Early Stopping: Training was halted automatically when validation loss stopped improving.
Data Augmentation: Pitch shifting and time stretching were applied to real samples to introduce variability.
Checkpoint Saving: The best-performing model weights were saved for final deployment.
The optimized model provided a balanced trade-off between computational efficiency and detection accuracy, making it suitable for deployment in a non-real-time inference system.

6.1 Evaluation Results
The DeepFake Audio Detection for Urdu system was rigorously evaluated to assess its accuracy and robustness. Testing was conducted using both authentic Urdu speech recordings and AI-generated cloned audios produced via popular text-to-speech (TTS) and voice-cloning tools. Limited Dataset Diversity: Authentic Urdu audio resources remain scarce, restricting coverage of dialectal and accent variations.
Model Generalization: While performance is strong on seen domains, extremely noisy or low-bitrate samples may yield unstable confidence outputs.
Computational Cost: Wav2Vec2’s transformer architecture is memory-intensive; GPU acceleration is recommended for large-scale or batch inference.

Language Scope: The system currently supports Urdu only. Expansion to multilingual settings requires retraining or cross-lingual adaptation.
Real-Time Streaming: The web app processes static uploads; continuous streaming detection remains future work.
API Dependency: Noise-removal relies on external ElevenLabs API; downtime or latency could impact preprocessing speed.
